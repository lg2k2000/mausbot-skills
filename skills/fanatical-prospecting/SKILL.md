# Fanatical Prospecting

## What this skill does

The single home for Jeb Blount's *Fanatical Prospecting* in Liam's sales work: field manual, Coach mode and Audit mode. The book's thesis: the number one reason for failure in sales is an empty pipeline, and the root cause is a failure to prospect. Prospecting means interrupting people who are not expecting you, across a balanced mix of channels, every day, with a short, relevant reason and a direct ask.

Adapted here for enterprise B2B. HPE accounts are large enterprises with long cycles, buying committees, contracts and budget windows. The book's mechanics (phone framework, voicemail framework, RBO turnarounds, email and text rules, Golden Hours and Power Hours, the 30-Day Rule) stay intact. Volume targets and in-person tactics are tuned for named accounts, not door-knocking.

This skill gets the conversation started. It does not run discovery (`gap-selling`), qualify the deal (`sandler-selling`), or map the buying group (`selling-with`).

# Ground rules for enterprise use

**Honesty (the book's own standard, made strict):** be transparent about who you are and why you are calling. Among the book's gatekeeper keys is "hold the cheese": no tricks.

**Excluded from the book (do not use or coach):**

- The calling-other-extensions hack. The book's example includes telling reception a misleading story about a transfer. Pretext is out.
- Walking in a back door and acting lost, or getting past security. Out.
- Calling again after someone says never to call. Respect every opt-out and do-not-contact flag.
- Texting someone with no prior contact. Text only where a relationship or permission exists.
- Bulk or blasted email. One to one only, as the book itself says.

**Enterprise tuning:**

- Qualify the buying window first. For contractual, budget-cycle, long-cycle, executive-level sales the book says qualify, then set the appointment. Typical windows: hypervisor or licensing renewals, hardware refresh and end-of-support dates, fiscal-year planning, data center moves, new leadership.
- Most HPE targets are conquest prospects. Use strategic bridges (researched, specific to one person and one account), not targeted bridges, for C-level and conquest names.
- Expect 20 to 50 touches to engage a cold executive. Plan multi-channel campaigns across several stakeholders, not one-and-done calls.
- In person means events, briefings, customer site visits, user groups and partner days, planned around existing meetings. No cold door-knocking at enterprise sites.
- Never invent a customer name, result, statistic, trigger event or quote. If a proof point is missing, leave a bracketed placeholder and say so.

# How it plugs into other skills

**`gap-selling`.** The bridge in a prospecting touch is a hypothesis about a problem. The first meeting validates it. If the touch lands a meeting, hand over to Gap Selling for discovery.

**`sandler-selling`.** The appointment Liam sets should open with an up-front contract. A prospecting call that drifts into a pitch breaks both methods.

**`selling-with`.** Build the stakeholder list for each conquest account from `selling-with` account maps, then prospect into several people at once.

**`jolt`.** Not a prospecting tool. If a maybe turns out to be a stalled buyer with an open deal, stop prospecting and load `jolt`.

**`elicitation`.** Once someone engages on the phone, use elicitation's prompts to gather information without an interrogation feel.

**`influence`.** The "because" research the book cites is the Langer study Cialdini reports. Use `influence` to check that every reason and proof point is true and every ask is clean.

# Mode A: Coach

Use when Liam asks for a script, a draft, a call plan or turnarounds. Pick the output that fits the ask.

**Phone opener (5-step framework):** one opener per target, under 30 seconds spoken, no pauses, ending in one direct ask. Label each step. Add a second version with an information-gathering ask for when the window is not yet qualified.

**Voicemail (5-step framework):** under 30 seconds; number twice at the start and twice at the end; only for conquest names or prospects near a window.

**RBO turnarounds:** for each likely reflex response, brush-off or objection, one Anchor-Disrupt-Ask line. Include the second-RBO fallback and when to move on.

**Prospecting email:** AMMO plan (Audience, Method, Message, Outcome) in 4 lines, then the email using Hook, Relate, Bridge, Ask. Subject 3-6 words, no question mark. Close per the email close rule above. Plain text, no attachments, at most one full URL including the signature.

**Text or LinkedIn note:** only where familiarity exists. Name and company first, 1-4 sentences, under 250 characters, no emoticons or abbreviations.

**Prospecting block or pipeline plan:** the day's Golden Hours and Platinum Hours, Power Hour blocks with outcome goals, list order from the top of the pyramid down, and a 30-Day Rule check (what was prospected 30, 60 and 90 days ago and what that means for next quarter).

**Always add:** 2-4 coaching notes (what to cut, where the because is weak, what would make the prospect say "so what?") and 1 next step.

## Speakable templates (fit before use; fill brackets with real facts only)

- **Phone, set appointment:** "[Name], this is Liam [surname] with HPE. The reason I'm calling is to set up a short meeting with you. [Real trigger or peer pattern, in their words]. I thought the best place to start is a short conversation to learn how you're approaching [issue]. How about Tuesday at 10?"
- **Phone, gather information:** "[Name], this is Liam with HPE. The reason I'm calling is I want to learn how your team is planning [renewal / refresh / migration]. I've found that teams who start [real reason] before [date or decision] have more options. Can you tell me how that decision gets made and when it starts?"
- **Voicemail:** "[Name], Liam [surname], HPE. [Number], that's [number]. The reason for my call is [plain purpose]. [One real reason to call back]. Again, Liam, [number], that's [number]."
- **Cold email ask:** "If it's worth a short conversation, you can pick a time that suits you here: [booking link]."
- **Warm email ask:** "When would a 20-minute call work for you this week or next?"

# Mode B: Audit (strict)

Use when Liam provides a call recording or transcript, a voicemail, an email, a text, or a sequence and asks for critique.

**Required output (use this exact structure):**

1. **What actually happened (facts only):** channel, target role, objective, outcome
2. **Objective check:** was the touch's objective clear (appointment, information, familiarity) and right for where the account sits on the pyramid?
3. **Framework check by channel:**
   - Phone: name, identify, reason, because, ask; any "How are you doing?", any pause before the ask, any talking past the ask
   - Voicemail: number twice up front and twice at the end, reason stated, reason to call back, length
   - RBO: was it a reflex response, brush-off or objection; was there an anchor, a disrupter and a second ask; did we argue or say "I understand"
   - Email: delivery (bulk, links, attachments, images), subject line length and form, Hook-Relate-Bridge-Ask, the email close rule, typos
   - Text or social: familiarity present, identification, length, tone, links
4. **Moves used:** with a short quote and what it earned
5. **Moves missed:** with a rewritten line
6. **Message test:** is it about them or about us (talk, tell, show, I'd love to)? Would the prospect say "so what?"
7. **Ethics and rules:** any pretext, invented fact, ignored opt-out, bot-sent outreach, or methodology named to the customer
8. **Activity and pipeline (if numbers are given):** dials, contacts, conversations, appointments; efficiency vs effectiveness; 30-Day Rule and Law of Replacement read
9. **Best next touches** (3-6, with channel and day)

Read full transcripts, never summaries. If speaker labels are unreliable, attribute by context and say so.

# Field Manual (Reference)

Chapter notes, figures and examples are in `references/chapter-notes.md`. Enterprise-adapted scripts for RBOs, gatekeepers and channels are in `references/enterprise-playbook.md`.

## I. The laws

- **Paradox of Basics:** the secret is obvious and simple, not easy. Superstars are relentless prospectors. The mantra: one more call.
- **The Universal Law of Need:** the more you need a deal, the less likely you are to get it. Desperation shows and repels buyers.
- **The 30-Day Rule:** "the prospecting you do in this 30-day period will pay off for the next 90 days." A slow month shows up a quarter later as what feels like a closing problem.
- **The Law of Replacement:** prospects leave the pipe at your closing ratio, not one at a time. At a 10% close rate, one win removes ten prospects. Replace at that rate.
- **Sales slumps:** almost always a failure to prospect. When in one, start prospecting; recovery takes about 30 days of daily activity.
- **Know your numbers:** Efficiency (activity per block) plus Effectiveness (activity-to-outcome ratio) equals Performance. Track by hand. "You cannot be delusional and successful at the same time."
- **The Three Ps** that block prospecting: procrastination, perfectionism, paralysis from analysis. Fix: one call at a time. Research C-level targets in advance, outside the call block.

## II. Mindset

Seven mindsets: optimistic and enthusiastic; competitive; confident; relentless; thirsty for knowledge; systematic and efficient; adaptive and flexible (adopt, adapt, adept). You control your actions, your reactions and your mindset.

## III. Time

- **Golden Hours** are for selling. **Platinum Hours** (before and after) are for research, lists, proposals, CRM, email and admin.
- **CEO mindset:** own your time, territory and database. Say no, prioritize, delegate, move admin out of the Golden Hours.
- **Time blocking and Horstman's Corollary:** work contracts to fit the time allowed. Block phone time, set an outcome goal, turn off email and devices.
- **Power Hours:** short, intense blocks of nothing but calls, with the list prepared in advance and notes logged after. Block the first one to two hours of the day for the phone (eat the frog); don't check email first.
- **Know your worth:** annual income goal divided by Golden Hours in a year gives your hourly value. Don't spend it on low-value work.

## IV. The four objectives of prospecting

Set an appointment; gather information and qualify; close a sale; build familiarity. For complex, high-risk, high-cost sales: appointment with a qualified stakeholder first, information second, familiarity third. When buying windows are contractual or budget-bound, qualify the window first.

- **An appointment** is only real when it is on your calendar and the prospect's, at a set time and place. "Call me maybe" is not an appointment.
- **Define the strike zone:** a profile of the ideal account (roles, size, windows, contracts, triggers). "Don't swing at nothin' ugly."
- Prospecting is a contact sport: no small talk, no long scripts; get to the point, ask, move on.

## V. The prospecting pyramid and the database

- Treat the database as a pyramid, not a square. From the top: qualified prospects moving into the buying window; hot inbound leads and referrals; conquest prospects (your best 10 to 100 accounts); prospects with known windows to nurture; prospects with partial data to qualify; the base with little data.
- Build lists by objective, channel, qualification, potential, probability, role, vertical and trigger. Start each block at the top. Ask whether this is the best prospect to call.
- Own the CRM: every detail of every interaction, logged.

## VI. The Law of Familiarity

The more familiar a prospect is with you, the more likely they are to engage. The book's averages run from 1 to 3 touches to reengage an inactive customer up to 20 to 50 for a cold prospect who does not know you. Five levers: persistent and consistent prospecting; referrals and introductions (give a legendary customer experience, then ask); networking; company and brand familiarity (let HPE's brand work); personal branding (speak in public). Social selling, layered with phone and email, adds to it.

## VII. Balanced prospecting and social

- No single channel. Balance phone, email, social, in person, text, referrals, networking, inbound and events by territory, deal type and tenure.
- **Social selling is not selling.** Use it for familiarity, research, trigger events and strategic campaigns; don't pitch in feeds. Five Cs: connecting, content creation, content curation, conversion, consistency. Send a personalized LinkedIn request after every meeting. Block 30 to 60 minutes a day, outside the Golden Hours.
- **Strategic prospecting campaigns** for conquest accounts: map decision makers and influencers, find coaches, build familiarity, give value first, and be invited in when the window opens.

## VIII. Message matters

- Asking for time is the hardest ask in the whole sales process. "Prospects meet with you for their reasons, not yours."
- Be quick, clear about your intent, and relevant. Lower the risk (a short conversation, not an hour-long demo).
- **Give a because.** A simple, true reason lifts compliance.
- **Bridges:** targeted (shared by a group of similar prospects, an educated guess) and strategic (researched for one person). Three kinds of value: emotional, insight (curiosity), tangible (logic). "People make decisions based on emotion first and then justify with logic."
- Test: what would make your prospect say "so what?"
- **Asking:** ask with confidence and assume you will get it; shut up; be ready for RBOs. Rule of thirds: get to yes, no or maybe fast.

## IX. Telephone

**Five-step telephone framework:**

1. Get their attention by using their name.
2. Identify yourself.
3. Tell them why you are calling ("The reason I'm calling is...").
4. Bridge: give them a because.
5. Ask for what you want, and shut up.

Don't ask "How are you doing?" "The moment you pause you lose control of the call." Avoid talk, tell and show.

**Five-step voicemail framework:** identify yourself; say your number twice; tell them the reason for your call; give them a reason to call back; repeat your name and say your number twice. Keep it to 30 seconds. Leave one when it matters.

**Timing:** a daily block beats trying to time calls.

## X. Turning around RBOs

- **Reflex responses** are automatic (not interested, we're happy, I'm busy). **Brush-offs** avoid conflict (call me later, just send some information). **Objections** are real reasons, usually with a because (a contract just signed, a locked budget).
- RBOs are finite; script and rehearse the turnarounds. "You cannot argue another person into believing that they are wrong."
- **Turnaround framework: Anchor. Disrupt. Ask.** Anchor buys your logical brain a second. Disrupt the expected fight with agreement or a question ("That makes sense. Most people aren't."; "I figured you would be"; "Tell me specifically what you are looking for"). Ask again for a specific commitment. After two RBOs, move on graciously and come back another day.
- Avoid "I understand". When the horse is dead, dismount.

## XI. Gatekeepers (executive assistants)

Seven keys: be likable; use please, please; be transparent; connect; hold the cheese; ask for help; change the game (early or late calls, LinkedIn, events, email, a handwritten note). For enterprise, EAs are allies; treat them as part of the account map. Salespeople help salespeople: a target's own sales team will often say who owns a decision, if asked honestly.

## XII. In person (enterprise)

Plan face-to-face touches around existing meetings (the book's hub-and-spoke): when Liam is on site or at an event, list the other stakeholders and nearby accounts worth a planned touch. Five-step in-person call: approach with confidence; identify yourself and say why you are there; gather information; ask for what you want; turn around objections. Be prepared for the meeting to become a real conversation.

## XIII. Email

**Three cardinal rules:** it must get delivered, get opened, and convert.

- Delivered: one to one, plain text, no images or attachments, at most one full URL, no spam words, don't hit many people at one company at once, scrub bounces.
- Opened: familiarity first (call, voicemail, LinkedIn, then email); short subject (3-6 words), no questions, about them.
- Converted: AMMO plan, then Hook, Relate, Bridge, Ask. No "Hi" in front of the name, no feature dumps, no "I'd love to". Proofread before sending.

## XIV. Text

Only after familiarity. Seven rules: identify yourself; message matters; be direct and brief; avoid abbreviations; use transparent links; pause and reread before sending; know your numbers. Best uses: following up a good in-person conversation within a day, a real trigger event, nurturing with genuinely useful links, congratulations.

## XV. Mental toughness

Four pillars: desire (what do you want, how will you get it, how bad do you want it); mental resilience (keep learning); physical resilience (exercise, sleep, food); feed your attitude. When in first place, attack yourself. "When it is time to go home, make one more call."

## XVI. Anti-patterns

- "I'm better in person" or any single-channel habit
- Calling the same stale deals instead of new prospects (the slump loop)
- "Is this a good time?", "How are you doing?", "I'd love to tell you about..."
- Pausing before the ask, or talking after it
- Arguing with an objection
- Long scripts, feature dumps, number-one claims
- Calendar entries that are really "call me maybe"
- Bulk email, links and attachments, question-mark subject lines
- Texting strangers
- Any pretext, invented fact, or ignored opt-out
- A bot sending, scheduling or sequencing customer outreach

## XVII. Checklist

**Before a block:** list built and ordered (top of the pyramid first); objective per name; bridge and because ready; RBO turnarounds rehearsed; distractions off.

**During:** name, identify, reason, because, ask, shut up. Anchor, disrupt, ask. Move on after two RBOs. Count dials, contacts, appointments.

**After:** log everything in the CRM in the Platinum Hours; send LinkedIn requests to people you spoke with; schedule follow-ups; check the 30-Day Rule.
