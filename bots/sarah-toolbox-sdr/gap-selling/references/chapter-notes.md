# Chapter Notes: Gap Selling (Keenan, A Sales Guy Publishing)

| Ch | Title | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Welcome to the Game | Know the rules of the sale: it's about the gap, not the product. |
| 2 | The Nine Truthbombs of Selling | Foundational principles (SKILL.md section II); Kanter's ten threats; the threat-o-meter; the PIC. |
| 3 | The Current State: Where Customers Are | Physical environment, problems, impact, root cause, emotional state. Technical vs. business problems (the police radio example: chains of command vs. gang violence and conviction rates). |
| 4 | The Future State: Where Customers Want to Go | Future physical environment, outcomes, impact, emotion. |
| 5 | Relationships Don't Matter (Kinda) | Credibility and expertise over likeability. |
| 6 | The Gap Defined | The gap is the distance between current and future state; its size drives value. |
| 7 | Get Them to Let You Help | The first job of selling. The yes test. RFPs are a beauty contest. |
| 8 | Discovery: Know Your Clients Better Than They Know Themselves | B.A.N.T. is bunk, the four question types, banishing open-ended answers, four layers of problems, the four qualifying questions, the CRM Challenge. |
| 9 | Is the Gap Worth It? | Do the math: value of the gap vs. cost of change. |
| 10 | Know Your Customers' Why | Intrinsic motivation. |
| 11 | How to Do a Kickass Demo | No discovery no demo (separate days), no ifs, stick to six features, anchor with affirming questions, demo map. |
| 12 | Move Your Deals Through the Pipeline | You're not the customer's b*tch (trials, ROSE); decision criteria, "I'm confused. You said..." (my four favorite words), buying process, focus on the next yes. |
| 13 | Troubleshooting | Prospect goes dark, stalled deals, objections (clarify the outcome, not the objection), three price objections, walking away. |
| 14 | Prospecting: Getting the First "Yes!" | Prospecting is selling the first yes: a meeting. |
| 15 | Smart Prospecting Prep | Healthy pipeline, know who you're calling, be a problem finder, think like a buyer. |
| 16 | How to Capture Attention | Intrigue (surprise, mystery, knowledge gap), an irresistible offer, practice round. |
| 17 | Set the Right Cadence | Use every channel, shake the message up, bug them without bugging them (8-12 touches). |
| 18 | Manage the Pipeline | Seven-question review, specific data, the next yes from the customer, the ski-school story, the Healthy Pipeline Checklist. |
| 19 | Build a Commit Culture | Commits within 15%; accuracy, not goals; challenge then accept. |
| 20 | Hire the Right People | Traits of gap sellers, starting with curiosity. |
| 21 | It's Not About You | Leaders keep the focus outward, on the customer and the team. |
