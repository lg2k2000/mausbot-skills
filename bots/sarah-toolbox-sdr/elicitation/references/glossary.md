# Glossary: The Truth Detector (Schafer and Karlins)

The book names sixteen elicitation techniques. The Ch 15 exam lists them, Ch 16 refers to "the sixteen elicitation techniques", and Appendix B defines all sixteen. Status in this skill: **Use**, **Use with limits**, or **Don't use** (see Ethics in SKILL.md).

| Tool (book's name) | What it is (Appendix B, condensed) | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Be a Skeptic | Light skepticism makes people add proof that they are telling the truth | Use with limits: only real, light skepticism |
| Bracketing | Offer a range of numbers or dates so they name the real one inside it | Use (keep it realistic; bracket toward the flattering side) |
| Cognitive Dissonance | Present ideas that clash with what they believe; the discomfort drives disclosure | Don't use |
| Curiosity | Create an information gap they want to fill | Use, as honest interest, never a trap |
| Empathetic statements | Mirror their physical state, emotion, or words in parallel language | Use |
| Erroneous misattribution | Credit them with a skill not associated with them so they correct you | Don't use |
| Feigned disbelief | Pretend disbelief so they defend and expand their statement | Don't use (genuine surprise is fine) |
| Hanging chad | Leave a thought unfinished for them to complete | Use with extroverts; never with introverts |
| Naïveté | People talk more to someone they see as naïve; they show their expertise | Use only when genuine (Ch 11: "Naïveté doesn't mean pretending you're stupid") |
| Presumptive statement | State information as fact; they affirm and add, or correct and explain | Use, as a hypothesis we will say is one |
| Quid pro quo | Offer information first; they reciprocate | Use with limits: never trade HPE confidential facts |
| Quotation of reported facts | Cite something "from a source" (the book: "whether true or false") | Use with limits: only real, sourced facts |
| Status manipulation | Ascribe higher or lower status; they justify or disprove it | Elevate only, honestly; never demote |
| Storytelling | A relevant story with a moral and a suggested course of action | Use, with real cases only |
| Third-party perspective | Frame facts in the third person; people say what they really think | Use, with real comparables only |
| Word echo | Repeat the last word or two when they pause | Use |

Used in the book but outside the sixteen: **allow people to flatter themselves** (Ch 2, Ch 15 answer key), the **presumptive question**, **negative presumptive question**, **tag question** and **escape clause** (Ch 5), and **try a friend** (Ch 12, ask someone else who knows).

Other terms: **Golden Rule of Friendship** ("If you want people to like you, make them feel good about themselves."), **friend signals / the big three** (eyebrow flash, head tilt, smile), **ego suspension**, **elicitation sandwich** (law of primacy and recency; recast in SKILL.md as courtesy, not camouflage), **pivot**, **spotlight effect**, **jigsaw puzzle effect**, **Bryan's loop** (counter-elicitation: "Yes plus," "No plus," or "I don't know plus," then redirect), **E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P** (MBTI preferences, Ch 13, Appendix A).
