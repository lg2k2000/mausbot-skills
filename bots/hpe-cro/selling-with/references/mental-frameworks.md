## 13 The Change Equation

Kathie Dannemiller: "Change = [ D x V x FS ] > R"

- **D:** dissatisfaction with the current state
- **V:** vision of what's possible
- **FS:** first concrete steps toward the vision
- **R:** resistance to change

Multiplicative: if any of D, V or FS is zero, resistance wins. It maps to the discovery roadmap: problem (D), process (FS), payoff (V). If a deal is stalled, ask which variable is failing. "Change resistance is rarely rational." Rory Sutherland: "The human mind does not run on logic any more than a horse runs on petrol."

1. **Dissatisfaction:** make problems specific, highlighted and prioritized (vague, hidden or ignored problems let resistance win). Use the written problem statement and have champions repeat it.
2. **Vision:** clarity, consensus and confidence about the future; contrast outcomes, not process. Test: would it make a press release headline, an earnings call or a board agenda?
3. **First steps:** a specific, approachable path (MAP, hardwired POC with go/no-go conditions) that accounts for group dysfunction, drama and past failures.

**Hyperlinked decision.** Make a first-time decision look like a repeat choice by tying it to a past decision the same person made that went well: "Even if it was manual, have you tried anything similar to this approach in the past? How did that work out for you?"

**Failure allergy.** In large companies, a change that fails is judged far worse than doing nothing. Make the first step small and easy to undo. If the buyer is stuck choosing, load `jolt`.

## 14 Trust: The Hidden Pipeline Metric

- **Trust hurdle:** set by each buying team; how much trust must exist before they commit. Push before clearing it and the deal goes closed-lost or you erase trust. Higher with a larger investment, higher perceived risk, bad past vendor experiences, more personal accountability, and weaker internal alignment. "The higher the hurdle, the more complex the sale."
- **Hurdle questions:** "How significant is this investment compared to others you’ll make this year?" "If you were to speak for your team, how “risky” do they feel this project is?" "What does the outcome of this project mean for you, personally?" "Does anyone have a different perspective from yours?"
- **Trust quotient:** "[ # Deposits (x) Size of Deposits ] / 2x [ # Withdrawals (x) Size of Withdrawals ]". Withdrawals erase trust twice as fast as deposits build it. "Trust grows at the speed of a coconut tree but falls at the speed of a coconut." Quotient above the hurdle means a closing position; below means not ready.
- **Deposits vs withdrawals:** a new idea vs just checking in; a narrative vs a feature dump; the full story vs withholding; reading nonverbal cues vs ignoring them; a compelling event vs discount pressure; nonobvious insights vs endless questions delaying a demo.
- **Moves:** let champions engage skeptics; make motives explicit ("The reason I ask…"); use disclaimers ("Would you mind if I ask, then you can decide if you’re comfortable discussing?"); check the starting point ("For my context before we get rolling, I’m curious what your impression of [Company] is?").

**Diagnostic:** *how high is the hurdle on this deal, and how close is my quotient to clearing it?* If the quotient is 5 and the hurdle 9, more pitching won't close it. You need trust-building actions, not selling actions.

## 15 The Non-Anxious Seller

63% of sellers struggle with mental health, triple the rate for the general workforce. Anxiety transmits to champions, prospects and team.

- **Superficial leaders:** hands-off, stressed at quarter end, sweeping directives and discounts; you leave anxious. John Heider: "The superficial leader cannot see how things happen, even though the evidence is everywhere."
- **Detailed leaders:** in the details, calm, clear on what changes next quarter; you leave feeling capable.

Test a leader: "Could you help me break down last quarter’s performance? I’m looking to understand what you’re finding is driving the team’s revenue." Then press: "What do you think caused that?" The same feel test applies to you from your champion's side.

Calm at the end of a quarter is the result of detail at the beginning of one.
