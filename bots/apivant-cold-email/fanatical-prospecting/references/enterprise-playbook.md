# Enterprise playbook: Fanatical Prospecting adapted

Scripts and patterns for Liam's enterprise prospecting (HPE large accounts; the same rules apply to Toolbox). Everything in brackets must be filled with real, verified facts, or left as a bracket and flagged. Liam delivers or sends every touch himself; nothing here is sent by a bot. Customer-facing text never names a methodology.

## 1. Buying-window qualifiers (gather-information calls)

Use when the window is unknown. One question per call; log the answer in the CRM.

- "When does your current [virtualization / support / licensing] agreement come up for renewal?"
- "What's on the refresh plan for [platform] over the next 12-18 months?"
- "When does planning for next fiscal year's infrastructure budget start?"
- "Who else is involved when a decision like that gets made?"

## 2. Phone openers

**Conquest executive, strategic bridge (appointment):**
  "[Name], this is Liam [surname] with HPE. The reason I'm calling is to set up a short meeting. I saw [real trigger: announcement, earnings comment, new role, data center move]. When [peer group] go through that, they usually run into [real problem]. I'd like 20 minutes to learn how you're approaching it. How about Thursday at 2?"

**Director or manager, targeted bridge (information):**
  "[Name], Liam [surname], HPE. The reason I'm calling is I'm learning how [role]s in [sector] are handling [renewal / cost change / refresh]. A lot of teams I talk to are [real pattern]. How is your team planning for it?"

**Existing customer (reactivation):**
  "[Name], it's Liam with HPE. The reason I'm calling is your [platform] reaches [real milestone] in [timeframe], and I'd like to get ahead of it with you. How about a short call Tuesday at 10?"

## 3. Voicemail (conquest or near-window only)

"[Name], Liam [surname] from HPE. [Number], [number]. The reason for my call is [one sentence]. [Real reason to call back: a finding, a peer pattern, a date]. Again, Liam [surname], [number], [number]."

Keep it under 30 seconds. Pair with LinkedIn and an email the same day.

## 4. RBO turnarounds (Anchor, Disrupt, Ask)

| RBO | Disrupt | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I'm busy." | "I figured you would be. That's why I'm keeping this short." | "How about 15 minutes next Wednesday at 3?" |
| "Not interested." | "That makes sense. Most people aren't when I first call." | "Would it be worth a short conversation to see if [real issue] applies? How about Friday at 11?" |
| "We're happy with [incumbent]." | "That's great. If it's working, you shouldn't change." | "Most teams I work with still like a second view before [renewal / refresh]. Could we compare notes for 20 minutes on Tuesday?" |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to. Tell me specifically what you're looking for so I send the right thing." | Then ask one qualifying question, and offer a short call to walk through it. |
| "No budget." | "That makes sense. Most budgets are set well ahead." | "When does planning for next year start? I'd like to be useful before then." |
| "We just signed with [vendor]." (objection) | "Congratulations, that's a big project." | "When does that agreement come up again? I'd like to check in well before then." Log the date. |

Rules: no "I understand"; no arguing; after two RBOs, thank them and move on; log the RBO and a return date; any request not to be contacted ends outreach to that person.

## 5. Executive assistants

- Full name and company every time. Be likable and polite. Ask for help honestly: "I'm trying to reach whoever owns [area]. Could you point me in the right direction?"
- Treat the EA as part of the account map; note names and preferences in the CRM.
- Change the game: an early or late call, a LinkedIn message, a short email, a handwritten note after an event.
- Never use pretext, claim a transfer, or imply a relationship that doesn't exist.

## 6. Email

**AMMO plan (internal, not sent):** Audience (role, familiarity, how they read). Method (standalone or part of a campaign). Message (their issue, in their language). Outcome (meeting, information, or introduction).

**Cold email (Hook, Relate, Bridge, Ask), close with booking link:**

Subject: [3-6 words, no question mark, about them]

[Name],

[Hook: one sentence about a real trigger or an issue their peers face.]

[Relate: one or two sentences showing you understand their world, through peers, not by claiming to be one of them.]

[Bridge: what is in it for them, in one sentence.]

If it's worth a short conversation, you can pick a time that suits you here: [booking link]

Liam

**Warm or existing-relationship email, ask for a time:**

[Name],

[Context in one line: last conversation, a milestone, or a change.]

[Why now, in one or two sentences.]

When would a 20-minute call work for you this week or next?

Liam

Delivery checks: one recipient; plain text; no attachments; at most one full URL (the booking link counts); proofread twice.

## 7. LinkedIn and text

- LinkedIn connection note after every meeting or event conversation: "[Name], good to meet you at [event]. I enjoyed hearing about [specific]. Let's stay connected. Liam"
- Text only where there is a relationship or permission: "[Name], Liam with HPE. Good talking at [event] yesterday. Would a short call next week work to pick up on [topic]?" Under 250 characters, no abbreviations or emoticons.

## 8. Multi-stakeholder campaign for a conquest account (example cadence)

Planned with `selling-with` account maps. Example over three weeks for 3-5 stakeholders (adjust by role and familiarity):

- Day 1: call plus voicemail, LinkedIn request, email.
- Day 3: call; share a relevant insight on LinkedIn.
- Day 6: call; second email with a different bridge.
- Day 10: call; contact the EA for the right owner.
- Day 14: call; text only if there's a relationship.
- Day 21: call; handwritten note or event invite.

Log every touch. Stop on any opt-out. Move to `gap-selling` once a meeting is set.

## 9. Daily block template

- Platinum Hours (before): build and order today's list from the top of the pyramid; research strategic bridges; draft emails.
- Golden Hours, first 60-120 minutes: phone Power Hour. Outcome goal set in advance. Email and phone notifications off.
- Midday: second Power Hour or half hour.
- Platinum Hours (after): CRM notes, LinkedIn requests, follow-up emails for Liam to send, tomorrow's list.
- Weekly: 30-Day Rule check and Law of Replacement math against the pipeline.
