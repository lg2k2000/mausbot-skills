# Mid-Funnel Playbook (Selling With)

Load on demand. Section numbers continue SKILL.md. Quotes are verbatim from the book; everything else is paraphrase.

## 16 Copywriting Shortcuts

"The sales rep who can write will outsell the one who can’t, 10 out of 10 times." Seven rules: 1 Lead with the truth ("Challenging a closed mind is a dead end."). 2 Obsess over your headlines. 3 Rephrase and repeat a single truth. 4 Choose your framing wisely (negative frames: "Save $300K per year" becomes "Avoid wasting $300K this year"). 5 Use a few golden phrases (At last. / Announcing, / If… then. / Get the… without the…). 6 Address common objections upfront. 7 Write to a single person. Answer the "WIFM" (what's in it for me?). "Drafting is about creativity. Editing is about clarity."

## 17 Forwardable Emails (Making Your Words Travel)

"You’re writing through your champions. Not to them." The champion's problem likely isn't their boss's, so the email has to carry both. About 100 words, linking to the one-page narrative, with no stack of case-study links.

Framework (exact): "Subject: Reads like their to-do list"; "[1] Open with a big, company-wide objective that affects everyone. [2] Insert a trigger phrase that’s familiar to your prospect’s leadership. [3] Use visual language to share the future and stir emotion. [4] Build tension. Suggest this future might not happen. [5] Align your ask with the decider’s job." The ask mirrors internal email: feedback, or an item on an existing meeting, not a 30-minute call.

Fallback follow-up framework: "[1] Reframe the problem. [2] Describe a logical approach and why they believe it’ll work. [3] Create tension (if we do this, good things; if not, bad things). [4] How you(r product) delivers on creating the good. [5] Call to action based on how your email needs to travel."

**Principles:** 1 introduce new information; 2 don't make buyers work for it ("Please don’t send me on a treasure hunt."); 3 address the immediate ("Speak to their greatest priority today, get prioritized on their calendar tomorrow."); 4 write 200 words, then cut to 50; 5 pass the weird test ("Would it be totally weird for someone else to read this message if I accidentally sent it to the wrong contact?" If yes, send it); 6 have fun with it; 7 ask your own team to critique it; 8 for executives, bottom line up front and short sentences. "costly signaling theory": the more a message costs you, the better it lands.

**Idea email:** "Subject: Idea, [Job to be Done] [Name], last we spoke, [their problem] was a big focus. I was just discussing [content/resource/tool] and thought of you. Still working on [goal related to the challenge]? If so, check this out [link]."

**Late-silence email:** "That it’s been tough to [functional problem] — which led to [strategic problem] — and left you feeling [source of internal urgency]."

**Account-team play:** pick a few late-stage deals, write the customer's current state with the CSM or AM, find one way to help the buyer today, have the teammate send it to you, then forward it to the buyer. Keep one exclusive, back-pocket offer (a sandbox, a sample report) for key logos.

## 18 Demos That Don't Suck

A demo is a champion's biggest risk: their boss is in the room. "Contrasting outcomes is a measure of value." "Contrasting process is a measure of change."

- **Scenario demo:** 1 confirm the cost of their current process, 2 pick one focused scenario, 3 show it in the future state, then stop, 4 call out the savings or outcome, 5 discuss concerns about the change. "Change feels far more manageable when it’s contained."
- **Reverse demo** (below-the-line users): find the one or two workflows they spend 80% of their week in, then ask them to demo it: "Would you be open to sharing your screen and walking me through what this looks like for you?" Show the same workflow in our product, then hand over screen control when it fits.
- **Above-the-line demo:** begin at the end. "Outcomes, not operations." Show the after state in the first 15 seconds and "Just enough product to create confidence you can deliver." Stage windows; tag-team with the champion. Pairs with `gap-selling` demo rules.

## 19 Why Buyers Hide Objections

Handling objections raises resistance, teaches buyers to "duck n’ cover", and creates "emotional debt". "the number of objections you hear directly relates to the trust you’ve built." First check whether it's a question (something missing or misunderstood).

**Understand, unpack, respond.**

1. **Understand deeply.** Buyers sit on a secrecy-to-transparency spectrum; the first objection tests the waters. Dig for the second-, third- or fourth-order concern (expensive, then over my approval limit, then I said I could approve it, then fear about promotion).
2. **Unpack the pieces.** "When this topic comes up, typically, it’s because of X or Y. Is either the case here?" Use connective questions ("When you shared… was that related to…?").
3. **Respond gracefully.** Invert ("Can I ask what a solution that’s too cheap looks like?"), challenge the assumption behind it, or add complexity ("Well, if you’re looking to maximize X, then you’ll want to focus on… but if your goal is Y, then actually, you’ll need to…").

Tone matters: a calm presence brings objections into daylight.

**Price follow-ups** after "How does that price range compare to your expectations?": "Is what you’ve seen about us interesting enough to keep exploring, despite the gap?" "Let’s say for a minute you wanted to make our pricing work. What would have to change?"

## Multithreading scripts (supplements SKILL.md section 09)

- "Who do you typically count on in a meeting to share a strongly held opinion?" "Is there someone you often turn to for advice on new projects?"
- Champion biases: "It seems like you want others to want this more than they do. If that’s a fair characterization, how do you think we could help them see what you see?"
- Sell with the spokesperson; coach them on what the skeptic needs to hear. "skeptics always hold more power than advocates."
- Triangulating buyer (blames the team for a no): "Gotcha. How are you feeling about that? Were you wanting your team to want this?"
- Pricing with two people, not the group: "I rolled both of these inputs into a set of contract options for us to look at. Could the three of us put our heads together on feedback this week?"

## 20 The Minimum Viable Sentence (MVS)

"Using the fewest words needed to move a conversation forward." Three parts: **Goal** (one shared objective), **Necessary context** (close the information gaps that cause bad replies), **Call to reply**. Example: "If we did a pilot this month, how would you share data on customer lifetime value?" Rules: one narrow goal per conversation, build one sentence at a time, use their viewpoint, data over stories ("a single metric can be worth 100 words"), one strong point ("A weak argument generally dilutes a strong one.").

## 21 Managing Up

Create internal allies with influence, then arm them with a narrative. "If you don’t create the narrative, someone else will." Weekly pipeline update: summary (closed vs goal; worst, base and best case with conditions), pipeline development (this quarter's discounted pipeline, next quarter's coverage, new pipeline built), this week's three priorities, three blockers. Talk in scenarios and dependencies. "Surface bad news early and often." Use 1:1s for high-leverage asks; break big asks into small ones; deliver two steps ahead; ask: "So, I’m wondering, what will you and leadership look at to determine if I’m in a good position for this?"
