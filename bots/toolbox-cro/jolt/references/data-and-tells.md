## Scorecard (Fig 3.2)

Four bipolar rows, each scored 1-7: comfort with ambiguity (1) to need for certainty (7); structured (1) to unstructured (7) alternative comparison; satisficer (1) to maximizer (7); procrastination (1) to decision avoidance (7). The total runs from 4 to 28. Over 22 means disqualify, or at least deprioritize and get manager scrutiny. Then add the exacerbating factors: time pressure and decision importance. The interactive scorecard, call-audit tool, survey and win-loss questions, interview guide, and coaching questions are on jolteffect.com and are not in the book text.

## Source tells: what the rep should ask themselves (Ch 3)

- **Valuation:** Do they want it all? Do they keep pressing on differences between options? Are they openly confused between choices? Do new capabilities distract them?
- **Lack of information:** Do they ask for more demos, references or experts than a typical buyer? Do they delay to collect more? Do they say they feel overwhelmed, "in the dark", or "still far up the learning curve"?
- **Outcome uncertainty:** Do they press for ROI projections and revisions? Do they mention past burns? Do they talk about how big a risk this is? Do they ask for guarantees? Do they doubt the result can be achieved?

## Depth tells (Ch 3)

- **Information:** "research for research's sake"; backtracking after late news. The book's story: a buyer reopened the evaluation over a Gartner Magic Quadrant report, and six months later there was still no decision.
- **Comparison:** one attribute at a time; can't explain the shortlist; "apples to Tuesday".
- **Standard:** after a pilot or workshop, ask how it went. Maximizers lead with the negatives ("Boy, getting the pilot going was a real bear for our team").
- **Delay:** a one-week push is procrastination. "Now's not the right time" or "priorities are shifting", plus a later quarter, is avoidance.

## Two playbooks, switched at intent (Ch 2)

The status quo playbook (the case for change) applies until the buyer says they want to change. After stated intent, switch to JOLT. Relitigating the status quo after intent backfires.

## Environments (Ch 10)

- **Inbound:** "an entire sales process shrunk down into thirty to sixty minutes". Judge within a minute or two. Hand misrouted service calls off with a warm transfer. Use guarantees, trials and flexible payment terms. Don't use FUD or "call back later".
- **Outbound and long-cycle:** committees, procurement, security and legal reviews. Indecision shows in every channel. Stop demos to gauge reactions. Don't over-probe. Set guidelines and expectations on more than 60% of interactions. Bolt on a services contract.
- **Account management:** renewals and upsells follow the same rules.

## Building the team (Ch 11)

- Good hiring pools include customer success staff, former customers ("They've done the job of the person they're selling to and they have personally felt the fear of failure"), and consultants. "Great JOLT sellers are as conversant in fears as they are in facts."
- JOLT is an overlay to the methodology you already use. Managers are the last line of defense against "happy ears".
